# Localisation: Spanish, Hindi, Portuguese, Arabic

> **Lesson 7.7** · All Audiences · 25 min

---

> **Creator Bonus** — This module is for engineers who want to teach system design themselves. It covers business and marketing topics, not technical content. Skip if you are here as a student.

---


English-language system design content dominates the internet. ByteByteGo, Educative, and the major interview prep platforms are English-first, and their international reach is limited by the assumption that engineering candidates at global companies speak English well enough to absorb complex technical content in a second language.

That assumption is increasingly false — and increasingly wrong. The global engineering talent pool is growing fastest in markets where English fluency is high enough for a job but not high enough for optimal learning: India, Brazil, Latin America, the MENA region, and Southeast Asia. A system design course that is genuinely accessible in Hindi, Portuguese, Spanish, or Arabic has dramatically less competition than its English equivalent — and the audiences are large.

This lesson gives you the practical decision framework for localisation: which languages to prioritise, what to localise (not everything), and how to do it without a full translation team.

---

## The Market Opportunity by Language

### Spanish

**Speaker profile:** 485 million native speakers. Major engineering markets: Mexico, Spain, Argentina, Colombia, Chile.

Engineering candidate volume: Mexico City alone produces more software engineers per year than the entire UK. Latin American engineers disproportionately target US-based companies (remote-first, USD salaries) and face the same system design interview requirements as North American candidates. Spanish-language system design content is severely underserved — the primary resources are poorly subtitled YouTube videos and auto-translated blog posts.

**Platform opportunity:**
- Udemy Latin America: Udemy's fastest-growing regional market. A Spanish-language system design course would face minimal competition at the category level.
- YouTube: Spanish engineering content has 3–10x less competition than English for the same keywords.

**Revenue potential:** At a $49 price point (PPP-adjusted from $79 USD for Latin American markets), a Spanish course reaching 3,000 students generates $147,000. That is a separate revenue stream with minimal incremental marketing cost once the translation is complete.

### Hindi

**Speaker profile:** 600 million native speakers. Primary engineering market: India.

India produces the largest number of engineering graduates in the world (1.5 million per year) and has the highest concentration of candidates targeting global tech companies. The FAANG/Big Tech interview pipeline is deeply embedded in Indian engineering culture. System design content in Hindi serves:
- Students and early-career engineers who are more comfortable learning in Hindi than English
- Tier-2 and Tier-3 city engineers who have strong technical skills but less exposure to English-medium advanced content
- Engineers at Indian product companies (Flipkart, Razorpay, Zepto, CRED) preparing for internal promotions that require system design competence

**Platform opportunity:**
- YouTube Hindi content for engineering and interview prep has massive audiences (channels with 500K–2M subscribers, monetising at Indian CPM rates but with enormous volume)
- Udemy India is highly price-sensitive; a Hindi course at ₹499–₹999 ($6–$12) may reach 10× the enrolments of the English version at the same or better total revenue

**Revenue potential:** High volume, lower price point. The monetisation strategy shifts: Hindi courses generate revenue through volume on Udemy and YouTube AdSense (Indian CPM: $1–$3, but view counts can be 10–50× higher than equivalent English content).

---

### Portuguese (Brazilian)

**Speaker profile:** 220 million speakers. Primary engineering market: Brazil.

Brazil has the largest tech industry in Latin America and a rapidly expanding engineering workforce. Like India, Brazil has a strong culture of candidates targeting US-based remote roles. Unlike India, Brazil's English proficiency among developers varies more widely — a Portuguese-language course reaches a segment of Brazilian engineers that English content simply cannot.

**Key difference from Spanish:** Brazilian Portuguese and European Portuguese are mutually intelligible but have meaningful pronunciation and vocabulary differences. For educational content, Brazilian Portuguese is the correct target (10× the market). A European Portuguese localisation is a separate project for a later stage.

**Platform opportunity:**
- Hotmart, the Brazilian course platform, is the dominant marketplace for digital educational content in Brazil. It operates on a model similar to Teachable but with Brazilians as the primary buyer base and better local payment method support (PIX, boleto bancário). A course hosted on Hotmart with Brazilian Portuguese content is entirely native-market.
- YouTube PT-BR: Substantial but less oversaturated than English system design content.

---

### Arabic

**Speaker profile:** 400 million speakers. Engineering markets: UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Morocco.

The Gulf states (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar) are investing heavily in technical infrastructure and employ large engineering workforces, both local and expatriate. The tech scene in Egypt and Morocco is growing rapidly. Arabic-language technical content at a professional level is exceptionally rare — the market is underserved to a degree that Spanish and Portuguese content is not.

The complexity: Arabic is diglossia — Modern Standard Arabic (MSA, used in formal writing) differs significantly from colloquial dialects (Egyptian Arabic, Gulf Arabic, Maghrebi Arabic). For written content (subtitles, PDFs), MSA works across all markets. For spoken content (video narration), dialect choice matters: Egyptian Arabic is the most widely understood across the Arab world due to Egypt's cultural influence.

**Revenue potential:** Gulf markets have high purchasing power (Saudi Arabia, UAE). A Premium Arabic course at $199 for Gulf engineers is fully accessible; price sensitivity is low. Egypt and Morocco are more price-sensitive ($49–$79 appropriate). The challenge is distribution — Arabic engineering candidates are not heavily concentrated on Udemy and are not well-served by existing platforms.

## What to Localise (The 80/20 Decision)

Full translation of 54 hours of content is not where you start. The 80/20 localisation prioritises the assets that have the highest impact on revenue per dollar of localisation spend.

### Priority 1: The sales page and key marketing copy

Your sales page (the page where someone decides to buy) must be in the target language. A student who reads fluent Spanish and finds an English-only sales page has a higher abandonment rate regardless of how technically fluent they are — purchasing is an emotional decision, and emotional decisions happen in your native language.

Sales page localisation: 1–2 hours of professional translator time (~$50–$150 per language).

### Priority 2: Course subtitles

Subtitles serve two markets simultaneously: non-native English speakers who can watch English content with their native subtitle support, and students in your localised market who prefer to watch the original English video with comprehension support.

Auto-generated subtitles (YouTube, Descript, Rev.com's auto-captions) are your starting point. They are 80–90% accurate for clear English speech. A professional review pass to correct technical terms (the word "kafka" should not be auto-corrected to "kafa" or "cafe") takes 1–2 hours per hour of video content at ~$20–$30/hour from a native-speaking technical reviewer.

**Subtitle priority order:**
1. Auto-generate with Descript or YouTube's built-in captioning
2. Human review pass for technical accuracy
3. Export as .srt file and upload to Teachable, YouTube, and Udemy

### Priority 3: The glossary and cheat sheets

The glossary (Module 0.4) and the cheat sheets (Module 5.7) are the most-shared assets in your content. Translated PDFs distributed as gated lead magnets on your localised landing pages are a highly efficient list-building mechanism. A student who downloads "Sistema de Diseño — Guía de Referencia" is a Spanish-language prospect who has self-identified.

Glossary translation: 4–8 hours of professional translation (~$100–$200 per language).

### Priority 4: Module 1 video content (beginner funnel)

If you are going to localise any video content, start with Module 1 (Foundations). It is the entry point for the widest audience, it has the most search volume in the target language, and it is what you can publish on YouTube in the target language to build a localised subscriber base.

**Localisation options for video:**
1. **Subtitles only** (cheapest, fastest): add human-reviewed subtitles to existing English videos. ~$30–$50 per hour of video.
2. **Overdub** (Descript feature): AI voice replaces your narration with a localised voice in the target language. ~$50–$100 per hour of video + AI overdub cost. Quality is acceptable for subtitled watching; not recommended for audio-only consumption.
3. **Human narrator** (best quality): hire a native-speaking engineer to re-narrate the lesson using your script translated by a professional. ~$200–$500 per hour of video. Reserve for a dedicated localised course product.

## Localisation Production Workflow

### Step 1: Translate the highest-ROI assets first

```
Week 1–2: Sales page + email opt-in landing page
Week 3–4: Glossary PDF + cheat sheet PDF
Week 5–8: Auto-subtitle + review for Module 1 (10 lessons)
Week 9–12: Auto-subtitle for Module 2–3 (selected lessons)
```

### Step 2: Launch a localised YouTube presence

Create a language-specific YouTube channel (not a translated playlist on your main channel — a separate channel). This allows the algorithm to surface your content to the right geographic audience.

Channel naming convention: `[Your Brand] en Español` or `[Your Brand] Hindi`.

Upload Module 1 lessons with localised titles and descriptions. Spanish example: "Qué es el Diseño de Sistemas? (Explicado con Analogías)" rather than a machine translation of "What IS System Design? (city vs building analogy)". Localise the title to how a Spanish speaker would actually search for the concept.

### Step 3: Price for purchasing power parity (PPP)

Charging $79 for a course in Brazil (where the average software engineer salary is $1,500–$3,000/month) is the equivalent of charging $500 in the US. PPP pricing is not charity — it is the correct price to maximise total revenue, because volume in price-sensitive markets compensates for lower per-unit revenue.

| Market | USD PPP adjustment | Suggested price |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico | 0.45 | $35 (MXN 600) |
| Brazil | 0.40 | $32 (BRL 160) |
| India | 0.30 | $24 (₹ 999) |
| Argentina | 0.35 | $28 (ARS 25,000) |
| Egypt | 0.35 | $28 (EGP 870) |
| Saudi Arabia / UAE | 0.85 | $67 ($79 in USD works) |

Udemy handles PPP automatically — their regional pricing algorithm sets local prices. For your own Teachable/Gumroad products, use a tool like Purchasing Power Parity (the `ppp.fyi` API) to detect a visitor's location and show localised pricing.

### Step 4: Find your localised distribution partners

Rather than marketing from scratch in each language market, partnership with existing creators in the target language is the most efficient path. A Hindi-language tech YouTuber with 200K subscribers who mentions your course (affiliate deal: 30% commission) reaches your target audience in days rather than months.

**Partner types to approach:**
- YouTube creators in the target language who cover programming/career topics (but not direct competitors)
- Engineering newsletter authors in the target language
- Bootcamp operators and coding educators in the market (B2B: license your content to them for use in their curriculum)
- LinkedIn influencers in tech communities in the target market

## Localisation Timeline and Investment

A realistic localisation investment for one language (Spanish, as an example):

| Activity | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Sales page translation + review | 2 days | $150–$300 |
| Glossary + cheat sheet translation | 3 days | $200–$400 |
| Module 1 subtitle generation + review | 1 week | $300–$600 |
| Localised YouTube channel setup + 2 videos | 2 weeks | $500–$1,000 |
| Partner outreach (affiliate setup) | Ongoing | $0 + commissions |
| **Total first-language localisation** | **4–6 weeks** | **$1,150–$2,300** |

Expected revenue uplift at 12 months: 20–40% of English course revenue, assuming good distribution in the target market. At $50K English revenue, Spanish localisation generates an additional $10K–$20K — a 4–10× return on the localisation investment.

## Language Prioritisation Decision Framework

Answer these questions in order to prioritise your first localisation:

1. **Where are your email subscribers from?** Check your ConvertKit/Mailchimp geographic report. If 15% of your list is from Brazil, Portuguese is your first localisation.

2. **Where are your YouTube viewers from?** YouTube Studio Analytics → Audience → Top countries. This tells you where your content already resonates — localisation amplifies existing traction.

3. **Where are your Udemy students from?** Udemy provides country-level enrolment data. A Spanish course on Udemy where 30% of your English students are already from Mexico, Spain, or Colombia means a Spanish version has a proven audience.

4. **What language do you have native-speaker access to?** If you have a professional colleague, co-creator, or contractor who is a native Hindi speaker and a software engineer, the quality of your Hindi localisation will be dramatically better than an outsourced translation from a non-technical agency.

**Default recommendation if no data:** Spanish first (largest addressable market, highest English-language competition, strong remote work culture targeting US roles), followed by Portuguese (Brazil), then Hindi, then Arabic.

## Closing Thought on Module 7

The monetisation playbook is not about extracting maximum revenue from your students. It is about building a sustainable business that lets you invest more in content quality over time, reach students in markets that English-only content ignores, and maintain the financial stability to keep creating without relying on a single platform, a single product, or a single language market.

The course you have built — 54 hours of system design content across 8 modules, 3 audience levels, and 15 complete designs — is a significant educational asset. The strategies in this module are the distribution and revenue layer that lets that asset fulfil its potential: reaching engineers at every stage of their career, in the languages they learn best in, at prices they can afford.
