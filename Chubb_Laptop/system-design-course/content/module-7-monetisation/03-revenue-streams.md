# 8 Revenue Streams: Courses, Cohorts, B2B, YouTube, Newsletter…

> **Lesson 7.3** · All Audiences · 30 min

---

> **Creator Bonus** — This module is for engineers who want to teach system design themselves. It covers business and marketing topics, not technical content. Skip if you are here as a student.

---


A single revenue stream is a single point of failure. The course creator who depends entirely on Udemy sales is at the mercy of Udemy's algorithm, sitewide promotions, and competitive pricing pressure. The course creator who has built eight revenue streams has a portfolio: some are evergreen and passive, some are high-touch and high-margin, some compound over time. The goal is not to operate all eight simultaneously from day one — it is to understand the full portfolio so you can prioritise by stage and systematically add streams as the business grows.

These eight streams are arranged roughly in the order you should build them.

---

## Stream 1: Self-Paced Course Sales

**Revenue type:** Passive (once built), transactional  
**Margin:** 80–95% (excluding platform fees)  
**Time to first revenue:** 1–4 months  
**Scale ceiling:** $30K–$150K/year for a single course

This is the foundation. Everything else either feeds it (YouTube, newsletter) or extends it (cohorts, B2B). The system design course you have built in this curriculum is this product.

**Revenue trajectory:**
- A course with 2,000 email subscribers at launch typically generates $10K–$20K in the launch week
- Monthly recurring sales (evergreen, no active promotion) settle at 15–30% of launch revenue
- Year-1 total for a well-marketed course: $30K–$80K

The key lever is launch frequency, not continuous promotion. Run one full launch per quarter (7-day promotional window, price incentive, email campaign). Between launches, the course sells at full price with no active promotion — "evergreen" revenue from SEO traffic, YouTube, and word-of-mouth.

## Stream 2: Live Cohort Programs

**Revenue type:** Active, high-touch  
**Margin:** 60–75% (your time is the cost)  
**Time to first revenue:** After first 100 course students  
**Scale ceiling:** $80K–$300K/year

A cohort is a time-boxed, synchronous version of your course: a fixed group of students who go through the content together, meet weekly over Zoom, hold each other accountable, and graduate as a class. The cohort model justifies 3–5x the price of the self-paced course.

**Cohort structure for a system design program:**

```
Cohort: 6-Week System Design Interview Bootcamp

Week 1: Foundations + Building Blocks (Modules 1-2)
Week 2: Beginner system designs (URL shortener, chat, Instagram)
Week 3: Intermediate designs (YouTube, Uber, payments)
Week 4: Advanced designs + deep dives
Week 5: Interview mastery + mock interviews
Week 6: Mock interviews + graduation

Deliverables per week:
  - 90-minute live Zoom session (recorded)
  - 30-minute office hours (optional, recorded)
  - Weekly peer-review exercise (students review each other's design)
  - Private cohort Slack channel (instructor available Mon–Fri)

Price: $499–$799 per student
Cohort size: 20–50 students
Cohort revenue: $10K–$40K per cohort
Cohorts per year: 4 (quarterly)
Annual potential: $40K–$160K
```

The economics work at 20 students minimum. Below that, the time investment is not worth the revenue. Above 50 students, the live interaction quality degrades — split into multiple cohort tracks rather than increasing cohort size.

**Finding your first cohort students:** Send a "founding cohort" email to your existing course students. Offer them the cohort at 50% off the regular price in exchange for detailed feedback and testimonials. This generates revenue, relationships, and case studies that make future cohorts easier to sell.

## Stream 3: B2B / Corporate Training

**Revenue type:** Active (sales process), then recurring  
**Margin:** 70–85%  
**Time to first revenue:** 6–18 months  
**Scale ceiling:** $200K–$1M+/year (with a sales process)

Corporations spend money on engineering education that individuals would never personally justify. A team license for your system design course costs $2,000–$10,000 — the price of one hour of an external consultant, but available to 20 engineers for a year. The L&D coordinator at a 500-person engineering org is not scrutinising your pricing the way an individual student is; they are justifying a budget line item against the alternative (external training vendors who charge $5,000–$15,000 per engineer per week).

**Entry-level B2B products:**

| Product | Price | Buyer | Typical deal size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team license (10 seats) | $1,500 | Engineering manager | $1,500 |
| Team license (50 seats) | $5,000 | L&D coordinator | $5,000 |
| Custom workshop (half-day) | $3,000–$5,000 | L&D or VP Eng | $3,000–$5,000 |
| Custom workshop (full-day) | $6,000–$12,000 | L&D or VP Eng | $6,000–$12,000 |
| 6-week private cohort (20 engineers) | $15,000–$30,000 | VP Engineering | $15,000–$30,000 |

**How B2B deals happen:**
- A corporate buyer discovers you through your course or YouTube
- Their engineers recommended the content to the L&D team
- They contact you about purchasing access for their team
- You respond with a team pricing page (Notion doc or simple landing page)

The first 3–5 B2B deals typically come inbound (they find you). Beyond that, you need outbound: LinkedIn outreach to engineering managers at companies where your existing corporate students work, referrals from happy students who are now managers, and partnerships with recruiting firms that prep candidates.

**The B2B case study multiplier:** When an engineer at Stripe takes your course, passes their interviews, and gets promoted to Senior, they are your most valuable marketing asset. Ask them for a written testimonial and permission to reference their company (even without their name). "Engineers at [Stripe, Airbnb, Google] use this course to prepare for system design interviews" on your landing page converts corporate buyers faster than any other social proof.

## Stream 4: YouTube Channel

**Revenue type:** Passive (ad revenue), active (sponsorships), evergreen (lead gen)  
**Margin:** 40–60% after production time cost  
**Time to first revenue:** 6–18 months (YouTube Partner Program threshold: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours)  
**Scale ceiling:** $50K–$500K/year at scale

YouTube is not primarily a revenue stream — it is a discovery engine that feeds all other streams. The ad revenue from a technical YouTube channel is real but secondary to the value of the audience it builds.

**AdSense revenue benchmarks for tech/education channels:**

| Monthly views | Est. CPM (tech niche) | Monthly AdSense |
|---|---|---|
| 50,000 | $6–$10 | $300–$500 |
| 200,000 | $6–$10 | $1,200–$2,000 |
| 1,000,000 | $6–$10 | $6,000–$10,000 |

Tech and education have among the highest CPMs on YouTube because advertisers (SaaS companies, bootcamps, cloud providers) pay a premium to reach engineering audiences. Even at 200K monthly views, AdSense alone is not a business — it is a supplement.

**The real YouTube revenue model:**

```
YouTube views
    │
    ├── AdSense: $1,200/month at 200K views
    │
    ├── Sponsorships: $500–$3,000/video at 50K+ subscribers
    │   (Scrimba, Interview Kickstart, LeetCode, Brilliant)
    │
    ├── Course sales (pinned link in description)
    │   → 1% conversion × 200K views × $79 = $15,800/month theoretical
    │   → Realistic: 0.1% = $1,580/month in course revenue from YouTube
    │
    └── Email list growth (gated lead magnets in video descriptions)
        → List growth compounds course launch revenue indefinitely
```

**Content strategy for a system design channel:**

The most-watched system design content on YouTube follows this pattern:

1. "How does [well-known system] work?" — Google Maps, Instagram, WhatsApp, Spotify
2. "Design [X] in 45 minutes" — mock interview format, always popular
3. "I passed my Google system design interview — here's how" — case study format, high trust

Start with 2 videos per month. Consistency for 12 months beats 8 videos in one month and then burnout. YouTube's algorithm rewards channels that publish reliably over time.

## Stream 5: Newsletter / Email List

**Revenue type:** Evergreen (launch amplifier), direct (sponsorships)  
**Margin:** 85–95%  
**Time to first revenue:** Immediate (if you have a list), 3–6 months to sponsorship scale  
**Scale ceiling:** $30K–$200K/year at scale

Your email list is the most valuable asset in your business. Unlike YouTube subscribers or Twitter followers, email subscribers are a direct line of communication that no algorithm can suppress. Every course launch email goes to 100% of your list — the open rate determines reach, but a 30% open rate on 10,000 subscribers (3,000 engaged readers) converts better than 100,000 YouTube subscribers.

**Newsletter formats that work for technical creators:**

| Format | Frequency | Value prop | Time investment |
|---|---|---|---|
| System design deep dive | Weekly | One full design walk-through | 3–4 hours |
| "This week in system design" | Weekly | Curated reading, 5-min format | 1–2 hours |
| Interview prep tip | 3×/week | One concept per email, very short | 30 min |
| Long-form case study | Biweekly | Full technical breakdown | 4–6 hours |

**Sponsorship rates for technical newsletters:**

| List size | Open rate | Typical sponsorship/issue |
|---|---|---|
| 5,000 | 35% | $200–$400 |
| 20,000 | 30% | $800–$1,500 |
| 50,000 | 25% | $2,000–$4,000 |
| 100,000 | 20% | $4,000–$8,000 |

Common newsletter sponsors for system design / engineering audiences: Retool, Temporal, PlanetScale, Neon, Mintlify, Interview Kickstart, Exponent. At 20,000 subscribers with biweekly sponsorships, newsletter sponsorships alone generate $1,600–$3,000/month.

## Stream 6: Consulting and Advisory

**Revenue type:** Active (your time), high-margin per hour  
**Margin:** 95%+  
**Time to first revenue:** Whenever you decide to offer it  
**Scale ceiling:** $100K–$400K/year (time-constrained)

Once you have established expertise through your course and content, companies will pay for your direct input. This is not tutoring — it is systems consulting: reviewing architectures, advising on technology choices, running internal workshops, or serving on an engineering advisory board.

**Rate benchmarks:**

| Engagement type | Rate |
|---|---|
| 1-hour consultation | $300–$600 |
| Half-day workshop (async prep + session) | $3,000–$6,000 |
| Monthly advisory retainer (4 hours/month) | $2,000–$5,000 |
| Architecture review (async, written report) | $1,500–$4,000 |

**How to get consulting work:**
- Be explicit that you are available: add a "Work with me" page to your site
- Your newsletter and YouTube channel establish credibility — include a brief mention once per quarter
- Students who become managers or principal engineers come back as consulting clients 2–4 years later
- LinkedIn visibility (posting content, commenting on engineering leadership posts) generates inbound requests

The consulting ceiling is your available hours. At 10 hours of consulting per month at $500/hour, you generate $5,000/month with minimal disruption to course work. Beyond 20 hours/month, consulting starts cannibalising content creation.

## Stream 7: Affiliate Partnerships

**Revenue type:** Passive, commission-based  
**Margin:** 100% (no costs)  
**Time to first revenue:** 30–90 days after application  
**Scale ceiling:** $10K–$50K/year as a supplement

You already recommend tools in your content: AWS, Kafka, Redis, Stripe, Datadog. Most of the companies behind these tools have affiliate or partner programs. When you recommend them and a student signs up through your link, you earn a commission.

**Affiliate programs relevant to system design content:**

| Company | Program | Commission | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| AWS | AWS Partner Network | Complex, apply separately | Primarily for consulting partners |
| DigitalOcean | DO Referral | $25 per new customer | Easy to join, good for beginners |
| Datadog | Partner program | Revenue share | Apply, approval required |
| MongoDB Atlas | Affiliate | Revenue share | Atlas free tier is widely used in courses |
| PlanetScale | Referral | Per signup | Developer-focused, good fit |
| Interview Kickstart | Affiliate | 10–15% | Relevant audience |
| Brilliant | Creator program | 30-day cookie, % of subscription | Good for general tech audience |

**The right way to do affiliate marketing:** Recommend products you genuinely use and would recommend regardless of commission. Your audience trusts your recommendations because you have demonstrated technical credibility — burning that trust with paid recommendations you do not believe in costs far more than the affiliate revenue.

Disclose affiliate relationships in your content (required by FTC regulations in the US, similar rules in EU and UK). A brief "this is an affiliate link" note does not hurt conversions for products you genuinely recommend.

## Stream 8: Digital Products and Templates

**Revenue type:** Passive, high margin  
**Margin:** 90–95%  
**Time to first revenue:** Days (once created)  
**Scale ceiling:** $20K–$80K/year

Beyond the course itself, standalone digital products serve buyers who want a specific piece of value without the full course commitment.

**Products that work for a system design brand:**

| Product | Format | Price | Creation time |
|---|---|---|---|
| System Design Interview Cheat Sheet | PDF, 10 pages | $19–$29 | 4–8 hours |
| Architecture Diagram Template Pack | Lucidchart/Figma | $29–$49 | 8–16 hours |
| 30-Day System Design Interview Prep Plan | PDF/Notion | $19–$29 | 4–6 hours |
| Interview Question Bank (100 questions + model answers) | PDF | $49–$79 | 20–40 hours |
| Mock Interview Scorecard Template | Notion | $9–$19 | 1–2 hours |
| Company-Specific Prep Pack (Google, Meta, Amazon) | PDF per company | $29–$49 each | 8–12 hours each |

The discovery model for standalone products is different from the course: they work best through Gumroad's native marketplace, a paid newsletter mention, a pinned tweet, or a "product recommendation" email. Each product is a lower-risk purchase for buyers who are not ready to commit to a full course — and once they buy a $29 PDF and find it valuable, they convert to the $79 course.

## Revenue Portfolio Summary

Realistic revenue at 24 months for a solo creator executing this playbook:

| Stream | Monthly (Month 24) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Course sales (evergreen + launches) | $6,000–$12,000 | 2,000+ students on platform |
| Cohort (quarterly) | $8,000–$20,000 avg | 4 cohorts/year, 25 students each |
| B2B / corporate | $3,000–$8,000 | 3–5 active team licenses |
| YouTube (AdSense + sponsorships) | $1,500–$4,000 | 30K+ subscribers |
| Newsletter sponsorships | $1,000–$3,000 | 15K+ list |
| Consulting | $2,000–$5,000 | 6–10 hours/month |
| Affiliates | $300–$1,000 | Passive |
| Digital products | $500–$2,000 | Gumroad, evergreen |
| **Total** | **$22K–$55K/month** | **$264K–$660K/year** |

This is not a guaranteed outcome — it is the realistic upper bound for a solo creator who executes consistently for 24 months. The median outcome is roughly half these numbers. The creators who reach the upper bound are those who publish consistently, build genuine relationships with their audience, and treat the business with the same rigour they apply to engineering.

---

**Key takeaway:** Build in this order — course first, YouTube second, newsletter third, cohorts fourth. Add B2B when you have testimonials. Consulting and affiliates are always-on; activate them as soon as you have an audience to speak to. Standalone products fill gaps but should not precede the core course.
