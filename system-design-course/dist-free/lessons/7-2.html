Pricing Tiers: Free → Pro ($49–$99) → Premium ($199–$499)

Lesson 7.2 · All Audiences · 25 min

Creator Bonus — This module is for engineers who want to teach system design themselves. It covers business and marketing topics, not technical content. Skip if you are here as a student.

Pricing a course is not about finding what the market will bear. It is about segmenting your audience by commitment, matching price to the value received at each tier, and making the decision easy rather than agonising. The most common mistake is pricing too low, which creates volume pressure (you need many more students to hit revenue goals) and signals to buyers that the content is commodity-grade.

This lesson gives you the tier structure, the psychology behind each price point, and the exact numbers you should test.

Why Three Tiers?

A single price point forces every potential student into a binary yes/no decision. Three tiers do something more useful: they let students self-select based on how seriously they are pursuing the goal. The student who wants to casually explore system design takes the free tier. The student actively preparing for interviews buys Pro. The senior engineer preparing for a Staff or Principal interview buys Premium.

This is the same logic that Spotify (Free/Individual/Family/Student), Notion (Free/Plus/Business/Enterprise), and every SaaS product with a pricing page uses. Price tiers are not about extracting maximum revenue from a single customer — they are about having the right product at the right price for each type of customer.

Tier 1: Free

Purpose: Audience building, trust establishment, lead generation.

Free content should be genuinely valuable, not a stripped-down sampler. The right question is not "what is the minimum I can give away for free?" but "what is the most valuable thing I can give away that makes people want to buy more?"

What to include in Free:

  • All of Module 0 (Course Overview & Roadmap)
  • The first 2–3 lessons of Module 1 (Foundations)
  • The Glossary (Module 0.4) — reference tools attract return visits
  • 1 complete system design walkthrough (e.g. the URL Shortener — Module 3.1)
  • The Cheat Sheet (Module 5.7 — latency numbers) — highly shareable on LinkedIn

Format options:

  • Free Teachable tier (audit access)
  • YouTube (full lessons, drives email list subscribers through lead magnets)
  • Newsletter (free weekly system design breakdown)

The lead magnet model: Your YouTube channel publishes a full 45-minute lesson. The video description says "Get the complete System Design interview prep guide (PDF) — free at [yoursite.com/guide]." That PDF is a gated download that requires an email address. The subscriber goes onto your email list and into a nurture sequence that leads to a Pro purchase. YouTube costs you nothing and builds the list that drives all paid revenue.

Tier 2: Pro — $49 to $99

Purpose: The volume tier. This is where most of your students land. High enough to signal real value; low enough to be a no-brainer purchase for someone with a job.

Price psychology at this range:

  • $49 is an impulse purchase for anyone with a professional income. Deliberation is minimal.
  • $79 is a considered purchase — the buyer takes a day or two but rarely involves anyone else in the decision.
  • $99 is a meaningful purchase. At this price you need clear social proof (reviews, testimonials) and a strong value proposition in the sales page.

What to include in Pro:

  • All of Modules 1–3 (Foundations, Building Blocks, 15 System Designs)
  • Module 5 (Interview Mastery Track)
  • Downloadable PDFs for all lessons (cheat sheets, architecture diagrams)
  • Private Discord community access (read-only or with posting rights)
  • Access to all future updates to included content

What to withhold from Pro (upsell hooks):

  • Live Q&A sessions with the instructor
  • Module 4 Deep Dives (advanced content)
  • Module 6 Globalisation content
  • Direct feedback on their mock interviews
  • Access to cohort groups (accountability partners)

Starting price recommendation: $79. Run a launch sale at $49 for 7 days to get initial volume and reviews. After 50 reviews, raise to $79. After 200 reviews and strong testimonials, test $99.

Tier 3: Premium — $199 to $499

Purpose: High-margin transformation. This tier is for buyers who want outcomes, not just content. They are not buying a course; they are buying a result — the job, the promotion, the compensation increase.

The value proposition at this price: A system design course that helps someone pass a Senior Engineer interview at Google (median TC: $350K) is worth thousands of dollars in outcome value. Even at $499, the ROI calculation is trivially favourable. Premium pricing is only unjustifiable when the outcome value is low — which it is not for technical interview prep.

What justifies $199:

  • Everything in Pro
  • Module 4 (Deep Dives)
  • Module 6 (Globalisation)
  • 3 months Discord Premium channel (direct access to you for questions)
  • 1 mock interview session with written feedback (async, submitted by video or text)
  • Private cohort access (next available cohort or on-demand)

What justifies $499:

  • Everything at $199
  • 1 live 45-minute mock interview session (synchronous, with real-time feedback)
  • Resume/portfolio review
  • Personalised study plan (10-day or 30-day, tailored to the student's target company)
  • Lifetime access to all future content including new system design additions

Price recommendation:

  • Launch at $199 to get initial Premium students.
  • After 20 Premium students and 10+ testimonials that mention specific outcomes ("I got an offer at [company]"), raise to $299.
  • Add the live mock interview and raise to $399–$499 once you have scheduling infrastructure (Calendly + Loom + structured feedback template).

The Psychology of Anchoring

Anchoring is the most powerful pricing lever available to you. When you show a student three options — $79, $199, $499 — the $499 tier makes the $199 tier look reasonable. Buyers anchor to the highest price and evaluate everything relative to it.

Without a Premium tier, the Pro tier is judged on its absolute price. With a Premium tier, Pro is judged as "significantly cheaper than Premium" — a relative position that feels like a bargain.

Anchoring in practice:

Present your pricing page in this order:

┌──────────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│       FREE       │      PRO         │    PREMIUM       │
│                  │                  │                  │
│       $0         │      $79         │     $299         │
│                  │  ← Most Popular  │                  │
│  • Modules 0-1   │  • Modules 1-5   │  • Everything    │
│  • 1 design      │  • All 15 designs│  • 1:1 feedback  │
│  • Glossary      │  • Interview prep│  • Mock interview │
│                  │  • Discord       │  • Study plan    │
│  [Start Free]    │  [Get Pro]       │  [Go Premium]    │
└──────────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────────┘

The visual position matters: Free on the left (low commitment), Pro in the centre with a "Most Popular" badge (social validation), Premium on the right (authority and aspiration). The centre position consistently outperforms the left and right regardless of price.

Pricing for Different Buyer Segments

Your students are not homogeneous. Pricing strategy should account for the segments you are actually reaching.

Segment 1: Student / Early Career (0–3 YOE)

  • Price sensitivity: high
  • Decision maker: themselves, on a limited budget
  • Right tier: Free → Pro at $49–$79
  • Triggers: "I need to pass this interview to get my first real job"
  • Do not try to sell them Premium — the ROI is real but the budget is not

Segment 2: Mid-Career Engineer (3–8 YOE)

  • Price sensitivity: moderate
  • Decision maker: themselves, professional income
  • Right tier: Pro ($79–$99) or Premium ($199–$299)
  • Triggers: "I'm targeting a Senior title at a FAANG/Big Tech company"
  • This is your primary revenue segment — optimise for them

Segment 3: Senior / Staff Engineer

  • Price sensitivity: low
  • Decision maker: themselves or their employer (L&D budget)
  • Right tier: Premium ($299–$499)
  • Triggers: "I'm preparing for a Staff/Principal loop, or I want to pass a system design round at [top company]"
  • Sell outcomes, not content ("join the engineers who got Staff offers")

Segment 4: Team / Corporate (covered more in Lesson 7.3)

  • Price sensitivity: low (budget line item, not personal spend)
  • Decision maker: Engineering manager or L&D coordinator
  • Right product: Team license ($2,000–$10,000 per cohort)
  • This requires a different sales conversation entirely

Practical Pricing Decisions

Recurring vs one-time payment: One-time payment outsells subscriptions for premium-priced courses. Students are wary of ongoing commitments for content they will consume in a defined period. Reserve subscription pricing for community/membership products (monthly Discord premium access, monthly newsletter) rather than the course itself.

Payment plans: At $299+, offer a 2–3 instalment payment plan. Teachable handles this automatically. Conversion data consistently shows 15–25% more buyers at higher price points when a payment plan is available. The effective price is the same; the cash-flow feels different to the buyer.

Annual price increase: Raise your prices once per year by 15–20%. Early buyers become advocates because they "got in before the price increase." New buyers perceive higher prices as evidence of growing demand. Send an email to your list 2 weeks before the increase with a price-lock offer for existing community members.

Lifetime access pricing: Include lifetime access to the current content by default. For new modules (major additions, not minor updates), you have the option to grandfather existing students in or charge an upgrade fee. Grandfathering builds goodwill; upgrade fees are appropriate for content that is genuinely a new product (e.g. adding a full live cohort track to what was a self-paced course).

The Pricing Launch Sequence

For your first launch:

Day Action
-14 Announce course to email list; mention launch price is lower than ongoing price
-7 Early-bird sale opens: Pro at $49 (limited to first 50 students)
0 Launch day: Pro at $79, Premium at $199
+3 Mid-launch email: share early testimonials, answer FAQs
+7 Price-increase warning: "price goes up at midnight" email
+8 Price increases: Pro $79 → $99, Premium $199 → $299

The early-bird/launch-price dynamic is not a gimmick — it rewards your most engaged audience (the people who have been waiting) and creates urgency that drives purchases from people who would otherwise procrastinate indefinitely.

Key takeaway: Start at $79 Pro / $199 Premium. Collect testimonials. Raise to $99 / $299 after 50 students. Test $499 Premium once you have live interview session infrastructure. Never discount your course more than 40% or you train your audience to wait for sales. Your Udemy course can go on sale — your main Teachable course should not.
