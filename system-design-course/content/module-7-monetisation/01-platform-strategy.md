# Platform Strategy: Udemy vs Teachable vs Gumroad vs Own Site

> **Lesson 7.1** · All Audiences · 30 min

---

> **Creator Bonus** — This module is for engineers who want to teach system design themselves. It covers business and marketing topics, not technical content. Skip if you are here as a student.

---


The platform decision is the highest-leverage choice you will make before your first sale. It determines your revenue share, your relationship with your audience, your ability to run promotions, and how much of your time goes to software rather than content. Most course creators choose wrong the first time because they optimise for discoverability (Udemy) when they should be optimising for margin and customer ownership — or they choose independence (own site) before they have the traffic to justify it.

This lesson gives you a decision framework with real numbers so you can choose the right platform for your stage.

---

## The Four Platform Archetypes

### 1. Marketplace Platforms — Udemy

Udemy is the world's largest online learning marketplace: 60 million students, 200,000+ courses, available in 75 languages. It provides discoverability at a cost.

**How it works:** You upload your course. Udemy promotes it to its audience. When a student buys through Udemy's organic search or promotion, Udemy takes 63% of the revenue. When a student buys through your own coupon or affiliate link, Udemy takes 3%. When Udemy runs a sitewide sale (which happens 20–30 times per year), your $199 course may sell for $12.99, and you receive $4.55 per sale.

**The real economics:**

| Scenario | List Price | Sale Price | Your Cut | Your Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic Udemy sale | $199 | $199 | 37% | $73.63 |
| Udemy promo sale | $199 | $12.99 | 37% | $4.81 |
| Your own coupon | $199 | $20 (coupon) | 97% | $19.40 |

Udemy's promotional model is a race to the bottom. The platform conditions students to wait for the next $10 sale. If your course is primarily discovered through Udemy's search and promotion engines, most of your revenue will come at $4–$6 per enrolment. The case for Udemy: it is the only platform that provides significant organic discoverability without requiring you to have an existing audience. If you have zero audience and limited marketing budget, Udemy gets you your first 500 students. Those students validate your content, generate reviews, and some will follow you to your own platform later.

**Best for:** Audience-building phase. Zero to first $10K. Never as a primary long-term platform.

---

### 2. Hosted Course Platforms — Teachable, Kajabi, Thinkific

These platforms host your course, handle payments, and provide a branded experience. You set the price. You own the student email list. You control the promotions.

**Teachable** is the most popular:
- Free plan: available but Teachable takes $1 + 10% per transaction
- Basic plan ($59/month): 5% transaction fee, email marketing, coupons
- Pro plan ($159/month): 0% transaction fee, grading, certifications, affiliate programs, priority support
- Business plan ($665/month): custom domains, removes Teachable branding, advanced analytics

**At Pro plan, the economics:**

| Revenue | Platform Cost | Your Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| $5,000/month | $159 flat | $4,841 (96.8%) |
| $20,000/month | $159 flat | $19,841 (99.2%) |
| $50,000/month | $159 flat | $49,841 (99.7%) |

This is the correct choice for the $5K–$100K/year creator. You own the student relationship, you set pricing, you run promotions on your terms, and the platform handles the infrastructure (video hosting, DRM, payment processing, email receipts, access management).

**Kajabi** targets the higher end: $149/month to $399/month, but includes built-in email marketing, landing pages, community, coaching products, and podcasts. If you want everything in one tool and are generating $10K+/month, Kajabi removes the need for a separate email marketing tool and community platform.

**Best for:** Established creators with an audience of 1,000+ email subscribers, or anyone willing to build that audience before relying on the platform for discovery.

---

### 3. Digital Product Platforms — Gumroad

Gumroad started as a tool for selling PDFs and design assets; it now supports full course delivery. The model is simpler than Teachable: no monthly fee, 10% cut on revenue (as of 2024, with the fee decreasing as you earn more).

**Gumroad pricing tiers:**

| Lifetime earnings | Gumroad fee |
|---|---|
| $0 – $1,000 | 10% |
| $1,001 – $10,000 | 7% |
| $10,001 – $100,000 | 5% |
| $100,001+ | 3% |

The appeal: no monthly overhead, simple product pages, pay-what-you-want pricing support, instant payouts. The limitation: fewer learning-specific features (no quizzes, no certificates, limited drip scheduling), less polished student experience, no built-in affiliate program.

Gumroad works well for **supplementary products**: a $29 interview cheat sheet, a $49 template pack, a $99 mini-course on a specific topic. It is less well-suited as the primary home of a $299 flagship course because students expect a dedicated learning platform (progress tracking, video player, discussion) for that price point.

**Best for:** Digital supplementary products. Audiences built primarily on Twitter/X, YouTube, or newsletters where a polished storefront matters less than instant purchase flow.

---

### 4. Own Site — Custom or WordPress/Ghost + Payment Processor

Building your own site means you control everything and pay 2.9% + $0.30 per Stripe transaction. No platform fee. Full customisation. Full data ownership.

**What "own site" actually means in practice:**

```
Option A — Ghost (recommended for creators):
  Ghost Pro ($36/month for Creator plan)
  + Stripe integration (built-in, 0% platform fee)
  + Membership/subscription tiers built in
  + Newsletter with 50,000 subscribers included
  = Total: $36/month + 2.9% Stripe fees

Option B — WordPress + LMS plugin:
  Hosting: $20–$50/month (managed WP)
  LMS plugin: LearnDash ($199/year) or LifterLMS (free–$299/year)
  Payment: WooCommerce + Stripe (free + Stripe fees)
  Video hosting: Vimeo OTT or Bunny.net (~$20–$40/month)
  = Total: $50–$100/month + annual plugin costs + ops overhead

Option C — Headless + Next.js + Stripe:
  You build it. Your team maintains it.
  Only justified above $500K/year revenue.
```

The own-site path makes economic sense above roughly $200K/year when the gap between platform fees (even Teachable's $159/month) and your time cost starts to favour custom infrastructure. Below that threshold, the opportunity cost of maintaining infrastructure exceeds the fee savings.

**Best for:** Creators above $200K/year annual course revenue, or those who run a subscription/membership model (where Stripe's 2.9% substantially beats Kajabi or Memberful's combined fees at scale).

## The Platform Decision Matrix

Use this table to find your starting point:

| Your situation | Recommended platform | Move to when |
|---|---|---|
| 0 audience, 0 email list | Udemy | 500+ reviews, start capturing emails |
| < 500 email subscribers | Gumroad or Teachable Free | You have 1,000+ subscribers |
| 500–5,000 subscribers | Teachable Basic or Pro | Monthly revenue > $5K |
| 5,000–50,000 subscribers | Teachable Pro or Kajabi | Monthly revenue > $15K |
| 50,000+ subscribers | Kajabi or own site | Own site justified at $200K+/year |

## The Multi-Platform Strategy

The most successful course creators do not pick one platform — they use multiple with a clear role for each.

```
Udemy
  └── Discovery engine only (no email capture possible)
      └── Students who find you → direct them to newsletter via
          "get the full course at [yoursite.com]" in intro video

Teachable / Kajabi (Primary)
  └── Flagship course ($199–$499)
  └── Email list lives here
  └── Affiliate program lives here

Gumroad
  └── Supplementary products ($29–$99)
  └── Quick-purchase items: cheat sheets, template packs

Your Newsletter (ConvertKit or Kit)
  └── The real asset — the email list
  └── Drives traffic to all three platforms
  └── Not a platform, but central to the strategy
```

The rule: never let a platform own your customer relationship. Every Udemy student should be encouraged (through free resource offers in your course content) to join your email list. That list is the only asset that cannot be taken away by a platform policy change.

## Practical Platform Setup: Teachable Pro Checklist

When you have 1,000 subscribers and are ready to launch properly:

- [ ] Create Teachable Pro account; connect your Stripe account
- [ ] Configure custom domain (course.yourdomain.com)
- [ ] Upload course sections in order; set drip schedule if applicable
- [ ] Create three pricing options: Single Payment, Payment Plan, Audit (free preview)
- [ ] Set up coupon codes for launch (30% off, 7-day expiry)
- [ ] Install Facebook Pixel and Google Analytics on your school
- [ ] Set up affiliate program (30% commission is standard for peers)
- [ ] Configure automated email sequences: welcome → lesson 3 → lesson 7 → completion
- [ ] Write upsell sequence: after completion, offer next product

## What the Numbers Actually Look Like

A realistic year-1 trajectory for a system design course creator with an initial audience of 2,000 email subscribers:

| Month | Platform | Enrollments | Avg price | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (launch) | Teachable | 85 | $120 | $10,200 |
| 2 | Teachable | 22 | $149 | $3,278 |
| 3 | Teachable + Udemy launch | 15 + 200 | $149 / $11 | $2,235 + $2,200 |
| 6 | All platforms | 40 | $149 | $5,960 |
| 12 | All platforms + B2B | 80 + 2 corp | $149 / $2,000 | $11,920 + $4,000 |

By month 12 you have $50–$70K in revenue, 600+ students, 60+ reviews on Udemy, and a growing email list that makes each subsequent launch more effective than the last. This is the baseline — Lesson 7.3 covers the additional revenue streams that layer on top.

---

**Key takeaway:** Choose Udemy if you have no audience and need discovery. Choose Teachable once you have 1,000 subscribers. Build your email list from day one regardless of which platform hosts your course. The platform is infrastructure; the email list is the business.
