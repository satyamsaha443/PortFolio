# Launch Strategy: Beta Cohort → Testimonials → Full Launch

> **Lesson 7.6** · All Audiences · 30 min

---

> **Creator Bonus** — This module is for engineers who want to teach system design themselves. It covers business and marketing topics, not technical content. Skip if you are here as a student.

---


A course launch is not an event — it is a sequence. The creators who generate $20K+ on launch day did not write an email at 9am and watch sales roll in; they spent 60–90 days building anticipation, collecting social proof, and positioning the product so that by launch day, much of the audience has already mentally decided to buy.

This lesson gives you the three-phase launch sequence: Beta, Testimonial Collection, and Full Launch. Each phase serves a specific purpose and feeds the next.

---

## Phase 1: Beta Cohort (8–12 Weeks Before Full Launch)

### Purpose

The beta cohort serves three goals simultaneously:
1. Get the course in front of a small, forgiving audience who will give you honest feedback
2. Generate testimonials and case studies before the public launch
3. Create a sense of urgency and exclusivity around the course

### Who to invite to beta

Your beta students should be people who trust you enough to participate in something unpolished — and who are genuinely in your target audience. Do not invite friends who will be kind regardless of content quality; you need people who will actually prepare for system design interviews.

**Beta candidate sources:**
- Newsletter subscribers who have replied to your emails previously
- LinkedIn connections who have commented on your system design posts
- Discord members from any existing community
- People who have bought a digital product from you ($29 cheat sheet, etc.)
- Twitter/X followers who regularly engage with your content

### Beta pricing and positioning

The beta is offered at a steep discount (50–75% off planned full price) in exchange for:
1. Completing the course within the beta window
2. Providing detailed written feedback (a structured form, not just "I liked it")
3. Writing a testimonial if they found value
4. Optionally, joining a post-course recorded interview you can use on your sales page

**Beta pricing examples:**

| Full price | Beta price | % off | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| $79 | $29 | 63% | Makes the decision easy; removes risk |
| $199 | $79 | 60% | Still meaningful investment, signals commitment |
| $299 | $99 | 67% | Founder pricing — used as a framing device |

Do not make the beta free. Free attracts people who are not serious; they do not complete the course, they do not provide feedback, and they provide no testimonials. A paid beta — even at $29 — filters for commitment.

### Beta size

Target 20–40 beta students. Fewer than 20 gives you insufficient feedback diversity. More than 50 creates more community management work than the feedback benefit justifies.

### The beta invitation email

```
Subject: I'm building a system design course — want in before anyone else?

[Name],

I've spent the past 4 months building a complete system design course 
covering everything from the basics (load balancers, CAP theorem) to 
full interview-ready designs for 15 real systems (YouTube, Uber, 
Google Drive, and 12 more).

I'm opening it to a small group of beta students before the public launch.

Beta students get:
  • Full access to all [N] lessons now (not later)
  • Founder pricing: $29 (will be $79 at launch)
  • A direct line to me for questions during the beta
  • First access to the Discord community

In return, I ask for:
  • Your honest feedback via a form (takes 10 minutes when you're done)
  • A testimonial if you found it valuable

Beta closes when 30 spots are filled — after that the price goes up.

[JOIN THE BETA → link]

Only 30 spots. First come, first served.

— [Your name]
```

### During the beta (weeks 1–8)

Week 1: Welcome email + Discord invite + "Let me know your biggest questions" survey  
Week 2: Check-in email — how are they doing? Any early feedback?  
Week 4: Midpoint survey — are they on track to finish? Any roadblocks?  
Week 8: "You should be done by now" email — request for feedback form + testimonial  

Host a 1-hour group Zoom call in week 3 and week 7. Record both. These calls generate genuine discussion about system design concepts, give you material for a future YouTube video, and make beta students feel like they got something no one else gets.

### Feedback form structure

Keep it to 8 questions — long forms kill completion rates:

1. Which module was most valuable to you? Why?
2. Which module could be improved? How?
3. Were there any concepts you felt were not explained clearly?
4. How much of the content did you complete?
5. Did this prepare you for system design interviews? How?
6. Would you recommend this to a friend or colleague? Why or why not?
7. What would you have paid at full price for this?
8. May we use your feedback and name as a testimonial? (Yes/No)

## Phase 2: Testimonial Collection (4 Weeks Before Full Launch)

### Why testimonials are your most powerful sales asset

An engineering audience is sceptical by default. They read sales pages looking for claims they can refute. The one thing that bypasses their scepticism is a testimonial from someone who sounds like them — same job, same background, same goal — who says specifically what result they achieved.

A testimonial that says "Great course, very helpful!" is worth almost nothing. A testimonial that says "I was a backend engineer with 4 years of experience who'd failed three system design interviews. I finished this course in 3 weeks and passed a Senior Engineer interview at [company]. The section on fan-out architecture was exactly what clicked the concepts for me" is worth the equivalent of a full marketing campaign.

### Collecting strong testimonials

**Ask specific questions, not "do you have a testimonial?"**

Send this to beta students who completed the course:

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you so much for completing the beta — your feedback was incredibly valuable.

I'm putting together the full launch and would love to feature your experience.

Would you be willing to share a short testimonial? The most helpful ones answer 
one or two of these questions:

• Where were you before you started the course (experience level, situation)?
• What specific thing clicked for you or surprised you?
• What happened after you finished? (Interview result, concept understanding, 
  confidence level, etc.)
• Who would you recommend this course to?

A few sentences to a paragraph is perfect. You can reply to this email.

If you're open to a 5-minute video testimonial (just your webcam, no editing),
I'll give you a 25% discount code for the next product I release.

Thank you — your name (or "Software Engineer at [Company]" if you prefer 
to stay anonymous) means a lot to the next student deciding whether to enrol.
```

### Organising testimonials by student segment

Your sales page should show testimonials from all three buyer segments (Lesson 7.2):
- Student/early career: "I had no interview experience…"
- Mid-career: "I was a backend engineer targeting Senior…"
- Senior: "I was preparing for a Staff/Principal loop…"

A prospective student self-identifies with the testimonial closest to their situation. Showing only one segment reduces conversion for the others.

### The results-before-full-launch milestone

Aim to collect:
- 10+ written testimonials (with permission to use name and role)
- 3–5 video testimonials (30–90 seconds each, embedded on sales page)
- 2–3 detailed case study write-ups ("Maria went from 0 system design experience to passing her Senior interview at [company] in 8 weeks")
- A net promoter score (average of "how likely are you to recommend on 1–10?" question in the feedback form)

If your average NPS from beta is 8+, mention it on the sales page: "9.1/10 average from 35 beta students."

## Phase 3: Full Launch (Launch Week)

### Pre-launch build-up (days -14 to 0)

**Day -14:** "Something is coming" email — tease the course without a price. "I've been working on something for the past 4 months and it's almost ready. I'll share more next week."

**Day -7:** Course announcement email — what it is, who it is for, what is included. Link to a waitlist page (simple ConvertKit form: "notify me when it opens"). Waitlist subscribers get 24-hour early access before the public. Announce simultaneously on LinkedIn, YouTube community post, and Discord.

**Day -3:** Behind-the-scenes email — share a specific story from the beta ("one of our beta students failed two Google loops before taking this course; here's what changed for them"). Include a quote from the testimonial. Build emotional resonance.

**Day -1:** "Tomorrow" email — brief, high anticipation. "Tomorrow at 10am ET, [Course Name] opens to the public. If you're on the waitlist, you get first access. Here's what's included: [3-bullet summary]."

### Launch day sequence

**10:00am ET:** Waitlist gets access. Teachable opens checkout. Email goes out to waitlist only.  
**10:00am ET:** LinkedIn post (longest launch post — value-forward, with testimonial quote).  
**11:00am ET:** Public access opens. Email goes to full list with launch pricing.  
**12:00pm ET:** Tweet/X + Instagram (if applicable).

**Launch email copy:**

```
Subject: It's live — [Course Name] is now open

[First name],

[Course Name] is now open.

[One-paragraph course description focused on the student's transformation: 
"If you're an engineer who freezes when asked to design Instagram — 
this course will change that."]

Here's what you get:
• [Key benefit 1]
• [Key benefit 2]  
• [Key benefit 3]

Launch pricing: $79 (goes up to $99 on [date + 7 days])

[→ Enrol now: link]

You might wonder: "Is this worth it?"

Here's what [beta student name], a [role] at [company], said after finishing:
"[Specific testimonial quote]"

The launch price of $79 is available until [date]. After that it goes to $99.

[→ Get access at $79: link]

Questions? Reply to this email — I read and respond to everything.

— [Your name]
```

### Launch week email cadence

| Day | Email | Subject line direction |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (launch) | Course is live; launch price | "It's live — [Course Name] is now open" |
| Day 2 | Social proof + FAQ | "Answering the top 5 questions about [Course Name]" |
| Day 4 | Student story | "How [first name] went from failing to [result]" |
| Day 6 | Price increase warning | "Last 24 hours at $79 — [Course Name] price goes up tomorrow" |
| Day 7 | Final hours | "Closing tonight — last chance at $79" |

### The "open cart" psychology

Limited-time pricing works because most people procrastinate on every purchase decision. The launch window forces a decision. This is not manipulation — it is setting a fair timeline for an offer and communicating it clearly. The students who miss the launch price are often your angriest customers; they are angry at themselves for waiting, not at you for closing the window.

Always honor the stated deadline. If you say the price goes up at midnight, it goes up at midnight. Creators who extend their "final hours" countdowns lose the audience's trust.

## Post-Launch: The Evergreen Engine

After launch week, you switch from active promotion to evergreen operation. The course continues to sell at full price through:

1. **Email welcome sequence** — new subscribers who come in through YouTube, LinkedIn, or SEO get a 5-email sequence over 14 days that introduces the course. Email 1: welcome + free lesson. Email 3: the problem (system design interviews are hard). Email 5: soft pitch for the course.

2. **Quarterly launches** — every 3 months, run a 7-day promotional window (10–15% discount, new bonuses if applicable). Announce to your full list. Expect 30–50% of your monthly evergreen revenue as additional launch revenue.

3. **Update announcements** — every time you add significant content (new system design, new module), email your list. "I just added a full walkthrough of designing TikTok's video recommendation system to the course. If you're already enrolled, it's in your dashboard now. If not — here's a link." These content update emails convert at 2–5% click-through to course purchase.

## Launch Metrics to Track

| Metric | Target | How to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Email open rate (launch day) | > 35% | ConvertKit / Mailchimp dashboard |
| Click rate on launch email | > 8% | Email platform |
| Conversion rate (clicks to purchase) | 5–15% | Teachable + UTM links |
| Revenue in launch week | $10K+ | Teachable dashboard |
| Students enrolled | 100–200 | Teachable dashboard |
| Refund requests (first 30 days) | < 5% | Teachable |
| Discord engagement (week 1) | > 50% of new students join | Discord member count |

### What low conversion rates mean

If your email open rates are high (35%+) but conversion is low (< 3%): the offer or sales page is the problem. Improve the testimonials, the price/value framing, or the guarantee.

If your open rates are low (< 20%): the list is cold or poorly segmented. Go back to consistent email publishing before the next launch.

If your conversion is fine but revenue is low: you need a larger list. Marketing (Lesson 7.4) is the only solution.

---

**Key takeaway:** Beta → Testimonials → Full Launch is a 12-week sequence, not a one-day event. The work you do in the beta phase directly determines how well the full launch converts. Do not skip the beta to launch faster — you will have lower conversion and less data to improve with. A well-run beta pays for itself in testimonials alone.
